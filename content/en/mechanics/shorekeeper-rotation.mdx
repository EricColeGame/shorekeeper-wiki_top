## Why Shorekeeper's Rotation Is the Real Damage Multiplier

Shorekeeper is one of the strongest supports in Wuthering Waves, and her value lives almost entirely in timing. A clean Shorekeeper rotation keeps your main DPS inside a healing field with Crit buffs rolling for nearly the entire fight; a messy one leaves you standing in dead air, re-casting a field that expired five seconds ago. If your damage feels lower than the guides promised, the rotation — not the build — is usually the culprit.

This guide breaks the Shorekeeper rotation into plain steps, explains which stats actually gate her uptime, and shows where she belongs in your swap chain. No spreadsheets required.

The core idea is simple: Shorekeeper is a *compressor*. She takes a handful of seconds on field, converts them into healing plus team-wide Crit buffs, and then hands the fight back to your carry. Every decision in the rotation exists to protect that hand-off.

## Shorekeeper's Kit at a Glance

Before optimizing a sequence, it helps to know what each button contributes. Shorekeeper is not a damage dealer — her personal numbers are modest — so every extra second she spends on field is a second your carry is not attacking. Her entire design is about compressing value into a short window.

| Kit Piece | What It Does | Role in the Rotation |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Attack chain | Spectro hits that also feed her Forte gauge | Gauge building between skills |
| Resonance Skill | Instant Spectro damage plus gauge and energy generation | Quick filler that keeps her on field briefly |
| Forte Circuit | Charged state that upgrades her healing and buff output | The gate you clear before the big cast |
| Resonance Liberation (End Loop) | Deploys the Collapsed Stellarealm | The centerpiece of every rotation |
| Outro Skill | Passes a Crit Rate and Crit DMG buff to the incoming resonator | The hand-off that ends her turn |
| Intro Skill | Entry damage and setup when swapping in | The start of her on-field window |

The single most important mechanic to internalize is that the Collapsed Stellarealm **follows the active resonator**. You do not need to keep Shorekeeper on field to benefit from her healing. Once the field is down, you can swap freely and it travels with whoever is attacking.

That one detail is what makes her rotation forgiving once you understand it — and punishing when you don't. Players who park Shorekeeper on field to "keep the heal up" are actively losing damage.

## The Standard Shorekeeper Rotation, Step by Step

Below is the baseline sequence that most teams can run without thinking. Treat it as a skeleton, then adjust for your specific carry's window length.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open with your sub-DPS or buffer | Applies their buffs and debuffs before the carry arrives |
| 2 | Swap into Shorekeeper with her Intro Skill | Starts her on-field window with free damage and setup |
| 3 | Basic attack chain plus Resonance Skill | Builds her Forte gauge and refills energy for the Liberation |
| 4 | Cast Resonance Liberation (End Loop) | Deploys the Collapsed Stellarealm — the healing and buff field |
| 5 | Use her Outro Skill to swap to your main DPS | Transfers the Crit buff to the character who needs it |
| 6 | Run your carry's full damage window | Healing and buffs tick while the carry attacks |
| 7 | Re-enter Shorekeeper before the field lapses | Refresh the Stellarealm and repeat the cycle |

### First Cycle (Opening)

On the opening cycle, your priority is getting the Stellarealm down *before* your carry commits to their burst. If your main DPS has a long, committed damage window, casting the field after they start is wasted value.

A practical order: sub-DPS abilities → Shorekeeper Intro → gauge building → Liberation → Outro into the carry. If your sub-DPS needs energy first, do that instead; Shorekeeper is flexible about being second or third in the chain, as long as the field is live before the big hits land.

### Sustain Cycle (Every Cycle After)

Once the fight is running, the Shorekeeper rotation becomes a loop: refresh the field, hand off the buff, let the carry work, then come back before the field lapses.

Community reports consistently note that the biggest uptime gain comes from *refreshing early rather than late*. If you wait until the field has already dropped, you lose healing during the gap and your carry eats chip damage. Watch the field timer, not your cooldowns.

### Rotation Mistakes That Quietly Cost You DPS

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Casting Liberation before building Forte | Weakens the field and shortens its effective value | Build the gauge first, every time |
| Leaving Shorekeeper on field after her Outro | Wastes your carry's damage window | Swap out immediately after the hand-off |
| Refreshing the field too early | Overwrites remaining duration for no gain | Refresh as it nears expiry, not on cooldown |
| Ignoring Energy Regen substats | Creates gaps where the Liberation isn't ready | Hit a sensible ER target before chasing Crit |
| Handing the Outro buff to a support | The buff lands on a character who isn't attacking | Always pass it to your main DPS |

Player experience suggests that the "leaving Shorekeeper on field" mistake is by far the most common — and the easiest to fix.

## Energy Regen: The Stat That Makes or Breaks Your Rotation

If one stat defines whether your Shorekeeper rotation feels smooth or sticky, it's Energy Regen. Her buff package scales with it, and her Liberation is what everything else depends on. Without enough energy, you simply cannot refresh the field on schedule.

Community testing generally lands on these rough bands:

| Energy Regen | What You'll Notice |
|---|---|
| Around 150% | Functional but tight; the Liberation may not be ready every cycle |
| Around 180–200% | Comfortable uptime in most teams, buffs noticeably stronger |
| Around 230–250% | The widely cited sweet spot for near-permanent field uptime and capped buff scaling |
| Above 250% | Diminishing returns unless the substats come for free |

A few practical notes:

- **Energy Regen is not wasted on her.** Unlike most supports, stacking it is directly productive rather than a defensive compromise.
- **Substat priority matters more than the main stat.** You want Energy Regen on your echoes, not just on the weapon.
- **Weapon choice can carry a lot of the load.** Her signature rectifier is built around this stat, and several free or lower-rarity options still contribute meaningful Energy Regen.
- **Echo sets worth considering** include healing-focused sets for raw sustain and energy-focused sets when your team already has enough survivability. Community reports favor the healing set for general play.

For a full breakdown of her official numbers and kit text, the [official Wuthering Waves site](https://wutheringwaves.kurogames.com/en/main) is the authoritative reference.

## Team Building and Swap Order

Shorekeeper fits into almost any team, but the swap order changes depending on who your carry is. The general rule: buffs and debuffs first, field second, carry last.

| Team Archetype | Example Cores | Shorekeeper's Job | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spectro hypercarry | Jinhsi + Zhezhi | Field plus Crit hand-off | Deploy the field before the carry's burst window |
| Fusion burst | Changli + Sanhua | Field plus Crit hand-off | Time the field around the carry's Liberation |
| Havoc sustained | Camellya + Sanhua | Field plus Crit hand-off | Long on-field carries benefit most from the healing |
| Electro | Xiangli Yao + Yinlin | Field plus Crit hand-off | Field uptime matters more than burst timing here |

Two rules of thumb:

1. **One healer is enough.** Running Shorekeeper alongside another dedicated healer usually costs you a sub-DPS slot and a chunk of team damage.
2. **The Outro buff belongs to your carry.** If your rotation ends with Shorekeeper handing the buff to a buffer, you've wasted the most valuable part of the Shorekeeper rotation.

If you're deciding between Shorekeeper and a more traditional healer, the trade is straightforward: Shorekeeper gives up a little raw healing for a much larger offensive contribution. In endgame modes where clear speed is the score, that trade usually wins.

## FAQ

**How much Energy Regen does Shorekeeper need for a smooth rotation?**
Community benchmarks commonly cite somewhere around 230–250% as the point where her field uptime and buff scaling feel complete, with roughly 180–200% being workable in shorter fights. Start by fixing your Energy Regen substats, then move on to Crit and HP.

**Should I use Shorekeeper's Outro Skill on my main DPS or my sub-DPS?**
Your main DPS, essentially always. The Outro hands a Crit Rate and Crit DMG buff to the incoming resonator, and a sub-DPS typically spends very little time attacking. Passing the buff to a support is one of the most common ways a Shorekeeper rotation loses value.

**Can I run Shorekeeper and another healer in the same team?**
You can, but you usually shouldn't. Two healers means one fewer damage dealer or buffer, and Shorekeeper's Collapsed Stellarealm already provides the healing your team needs. The exception is a fight where survival is genuinely the bottleneck.

**Does the Shorekeeper rotation change in Tower of Adversity or other endgame modes?**
The structure stays the same — build gauge, deploy the field, hand off the buff — but the tempo tightens. In timed content you have less room to refresh early, so hitting your Energy Regen target becomes more important than ever. Practice the loop in the overworld until the hand-off is automatic.

**What's the single biggest mistake in a Shorekeeper rotation?**
Staying on field too long. Once the Stellarealm is deployed and the Outro buff is passed, Shorekeeper has done her job. Every additional second she spends attacking is damage your carry didn't deal.
