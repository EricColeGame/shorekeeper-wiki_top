### How to Verify You Have the Real Thing

Player experience in the community consistently points to a few quick checks:

- **Look for a source link.** Official posts trace back to a verified publisher account or the official site. A repost usually does not.
- **Check the edges.** Genuine master files have clean, intentional cropping. Reposts often show hard-cut borders, leftover UI, or a second watermark.
- **Zoom to 200%.** AI upscaling smears fine detail — fabric stitching, hair strands, and thin linework turn to mush.
- **Compare colors.** Recompressed images drift toward muddy blues. The official palette stays crisp and cool.
- **Check the aspect ratio.** If a "poster" is 16:9 with a stretched subject, it was probably cropped from a banner.

### Official Art vs. Fan Art: Know the Difference

| Feature | Official art | Fan art |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Publisher and studio channels | Independent artists |
| Resolution | Master files, print grade | Varies widely |
| Consistency | Matches the in-game model and lore | Stylized interpretations |
| Usage rights | Usually personal use only | Set by each artist |
| Best for | Wallpapers, references, wikis | Personal collections, commissions |

A quick note on rights: even official artwork is generally licensed for personal, non-commercial use. Slapping a key visual on merchandise you sell is a different legal situation entirely. If you want art for a project you plan to publish or sell, commission a fan artist and respect their terms — that is both safer and better for the community.

**Is Shorekeeper official art free to use?**

**How do I tell official Shorekeeper official art from an AI upscale or a repost?**

**Does the Shorekeeper have more than one official illustration?**
